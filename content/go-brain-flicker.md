Brain is complicated, human body is complicated, and most of these complications we get from web of causality that connects organs to one another. When we get too much sugar in blood then pancreas produces insulin -> this is one of these simpler mechanism. But looking closer, we get some non clear observations - like -> how is it possible that we get very tight feedback loop so we have almost always same range of blood glucose?

Complicated systems have one characteristic advantage - or disadvantage, they are hackable by their nature. By hackable I mean that we can use non standard combinations of inputs to get something that is normally inaccessible. Like speedruner, that by using series of steps that look nonsensical to onlooker can skip levels.

Most hacks, that could be easily used -> caffeine, alcohol, meditation - are all in this category. But there are more recent ones that look like magic, for example "God helmet", or brain stimulation devices for Parkinson - they are possible by recent science advances.

One of these recent hacks, is something that looks deeply connected to sleep and Alzheimer. USA firm [Cognito therapeutics](https://www.cognitotx.com/)  develops commercial version, and studies are looking promising [^1], but they are still in progress. We will look at amateur version, based on one of studies  [^2] that inspired this product.

Most important stuff from this study - they used specific pattern, visible below, that flickered with specific frequency, in burst. It looks like one of hypnosis devices pictures, and by flicking it in 4hz frequency we get basic effect. Some other studies also added sound to this combination, to better induce these cleaning states.
![[_resources/BRAIN CLEARNING OR NONSTANDARD INVERVENTIONS/7ee1368b2acce331b4ad3323401f0497_MD5.jpeg]]

So what can we do ourselves?  We can use this [webpage](https://www.paulkeeble.co.uk/cff/neural3) for 5 minutes per day, where we are presented with above pattern, flicking with 4hz in 16 seconds burst - this, according to this study [ Neural activity induced by sensory stimulation can drive large-scale cerebrospinal fluid flow during wakefulness in humans](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.3002035), can induce these cleaning states, that potentially  help with brain diseases.

There is also app version that you can download [here](https://github.com/2bits2bits2/go-brain-flicker/releases/tag/v0.1) , for someone who prefers desktop applications.
